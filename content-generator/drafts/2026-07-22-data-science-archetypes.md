# Data science jobs are bifurcating into two archetypes — which one are you building toward?

**Pillar:** Career growth in big tech
**Status:** Approved — draft only, not published
**Date:** 2026-07-22
**Word count:** 230

---

"Data scientist" stopped being one job years ago. Most resumes just haven't caught up.

I've reviewed hundreds of resumes from Prep Vector students this year. I can usually tell within a few lines which of two jobs someone's actually going after, even when their title looks the same as everyone else's.

→ Builder: builds models, ships them, scales them. Success = systems that work.
→ Translator: runs experiments, digs into causal inference, earns trust from leadership. Success = decisions people actually act on.

Facebook ran into this years ago. The "data scientist" role got confusing enough that they split off a separate Core Data Science group for research and applied ML.

That was the industry quietly admitting one title had become two jobs.

The numbers back it up. 77% of DS postings now ask for ML skills. NLP demand went from 5% to 19% of postings in a single year.

Generalist roles are shrinking. Specialized, deployment-ready talent is getting harder to find.

Here's what I think: the industry treats Builder as the "real" DS job and Translator as second best. I think that's wrong. Translator work is harder to hire for, and it's harder for AI to replace.

Look at your last three projects. Did you spend more time shipping models, or shaping decisions people trusted?

That's your track, whether you meant to pick it or not.

Which one is your resume actually saying?
